# Does semantic valence induce a halo effect on facial attractiveness? A Diffusion Decision Model approach

**Authors: Angely Charles and Alexandre Coumes
Institution: Phelma, Grenoble INP, University Grenoble Alpes, France**

This project explores the impact of semantic information (such as personal descriptions) on the evaluation of facial attractiveness, particularly in the context of dating apps where users make split-second binary choices. Specifically, we investigate whether a positive description can make a face seem more attractive.
To understand the cognitive mechanisms driving these decisions, we applied the Diffusion Decision Model (DDM) to analyze reaction times and choices. This allowed us to determine whether semantic context biases our initial readiness to decide (starting point) or whether it changes how fast we process visual evidence (drift rate).

## Methodology :
- Task: Participants were asked to quickly indicate whether a face presented on the screen was attractive or unattractive (speeded binary decision).
- Stimuli: In each trial, a word with positive, negative, or neutral semantic valence was presented just before the face.
- Databases: Face stimuli were selected from the Chicago Face Database, and the semantic words were sourced from the Emotional Norms database.

## Results :
- The analysis revealed that reaction times differed significantly depending on whether faces were categorized as attractive or unattractive.
- There was a significant interaction between the face's attractiveness label and the preceding word's valence.
- For instance, unattractive faces were judged significantly faster when preceded by a negative description.
- From a cognitive modeling perspective (DDM), "incongruent" trials (e.g., a positive word followed by an unattractive face) produced a greater boundary separation, indicating that participants adopted a more cautious decision policy when facing a visual-semantic conflict.
- Incongruence also reduced the drift rate, suggesting a less efficient evidence accumulation process during decision formation.
